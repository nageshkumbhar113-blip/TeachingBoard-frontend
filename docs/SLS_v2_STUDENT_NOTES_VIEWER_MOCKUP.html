1. Introduction to DNA Replication

DNA replication is the process by which a double-stranded DNA molecule is copied to produce two identical DNA molecules. This is a semi-conservative process where each new DNA molecule contains one original strand and one newly synthesized strand. This process is essential for cell division and the transmission of genetic information from one generation to the next.

🔑 Key Points

  • Occurs during S phase of cell cycle
  • Semi-conservative: each new molecule has one old + one new strand
  • Produces exact copies of genetic material
  • Initiated at specific sites called origins of replication

2. Base Pairing Rules

Each base pair is connected by hydrogen bonds: A-T forms 2 hydrogen bonds, while G-C forms 3 hydrogen bonds. This is why GC-rich DNA regions are more thermally stable. The specific pairing (A-T, G-C only) ensures that when DNA is copied, each new strand is an exact match of the original.

⚠️ Important Note

The strength of hydrogen bonds in G-C pairs makes them harder to denature. This is crucial for understanding DNA stability in different organisms and conditions.

3. DNA Replication Process

Before a cell divides, the double helix unwinds and an enzyme called DNA polymerase synthesizes new strands. The process can be broken down into several key stages:

🔑 Stages of Replication

  • Initiation: DNA helicase unwinds the double helix at origins of replication
  • Elongation: DNA polymerase adds complementary nucleotides to the template strand
  • Leading Strand: Synthesized continuously in 5' to 3' direction
  • Lagging Strand: Synthesized in short fragments called Okazaki fragments
  • Termination: DNA ligase seals nicks between fragments

The replication fork is the Y-shaped structure where DNA unwinds and new strands are synthesized. This fork moves along the DNA molecule at a rate of approximately 1000 nucleotides per second in bacteria and 50 nucleotides per second in eukaryotes.

4. Key Enzymes Involved

Main Enzymes:
DNA Helicase | DNA Primase | DNA Polymerase III | DNA Polymerase I | DNA Ligase

Each enzyme has a specific role. DNA Helicase breaks the hydrogen bonds and unwinds the helix. DNA Polymerase adds nucleotides, while DNA Ligase seals the sugar-phosphate backbone.

📝 Summary

DNA replication is a semi-conservative process that creates two identical copies of DNA through the action of multiple enzymes. The double helix unwinds, base pairing rules ensure accuracy, and complementary strands are synthesized. This process is fundamental to all life as it allows genetic information to be passed on accurately to daughter cells.
